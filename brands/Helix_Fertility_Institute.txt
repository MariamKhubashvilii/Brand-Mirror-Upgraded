Medically sensitive facts (for grounding article content)
Live birth rates per embryo transfer are highest for patients under 35 (national aggregate figures cluster around 40-50%+) and decline with age through the late 30s and more sharply after 40, per CDC's national ART data. For egg freezing, live birth chance per thawed egg runs roughly 5-7% depending on age at freezing, with overall live birth odds (using a full batch of frozen eggs) around 50-60% if frozen before 35, dropping notably after that; ASRM generally advises against delaying elective egg freezing much past 38. Nationally, patients need an average of about 2.3 IVF cycles to achieve a live birth. All of this should carry an explicit caveat in any generated copy: these are national aggregate figures, not predictions for any individual, and actual outcomes depend on diagnosis, age, egg/sperm quality, and uterine factors, a doctor should always be consulted before treatment decisions.
